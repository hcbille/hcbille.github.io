This year I've made a point of reading more.
And somehow, these last few weeks have seen multiple books land on my doorstep.

## Summer Eternal: Anthology One

Summer Eternal is a games studio founded by some former ZA/UM developers, the brains behind Disco Elysium. 
Its organisational structure feels like something we need in 2026: it's a worker- and player-owned collective, which shapes a lot of what this book is. This book reads as part manifesto and part essays about the video games industry. It was also the announcement of their first game, *Red Rooster*. Where most studios would have put out a trailer, they put out a book.

It mixes concept art, worldbuilding for the setting of Arta-Tantor, design writing. 

My copy also came with a 10-inch vinyl featuring music written for the game by Corvad and Nemeček.

I haven't finished it yet, it's 500+ pages. But it's a very interesting read and exactly what I needed.

### The reason as to why I think it's important

I  see this book and this studio as extremely important right now, when most of gaming is getting swallowed up by VC money and creativity is getting lost.

The pages echo Rockstar Games' betrayal of the workers who tried to start a union and got fired. We need a future that keeps the creativity within gaming.

## Public Access Afterworld

*Public Access Afterworld* is the debut novel from Jane Schoenbrun, the filmmaker behind *We're All Going to the World's Fair* and *I Saw the TV Glow*. Schoenbrun calls it the final part of their "screen trilogy", and it was originally written as a TV series before being reworked into this book.

It's an anthology that centers around a secret television network across decades. Part of the story is told through David and Erin, two teenagers who become obsessed with a mysterious analog phenomenon called Public Access Afterworld. The other part follows Bethany, a young trans woman working as a content moderator for a YouTube-like platform, who gets pulled into the same mystery.

It's a mix of horror, sci-fi, fantasy and coming-of-age story, and at 608 pages it's following the pattern from Summer Eternal.

I picked it up at release after loving their movie *I Saw the TV Glow* and it's horror in the exact same way. 

I've made it around 100 pages thus far and it's great. I feel a deep connection to the characters thus far!
